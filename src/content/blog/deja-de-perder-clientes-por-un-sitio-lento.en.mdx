## The hidden reasons behind a slow-loading webpage

It might shock you just how many factors can quietly drag down your page
speed. If your website is lagging, it's usually a combination of these
culprits:

- **Bloated page size:** too many heavy scripts and unnecessary elements
  running in the background.
- **Subpar web hosting:** a weak server that can't handle your traffic volume.
- **Unoptimized media:** massive, uncompressed image and video files.
- **Lack of mobile optimization:** a site that looks fine on a desktop but
  completely falls apart on a smartphone.

We have all experienced the frustration of clicking a link only to stare at a
blank screen or a half-rendered page. When customers are in a hurry looking
for quick answers or products, they won't wait around. A slow load speed
guarantees they will look elsewhere.

## The technical reality: Google's Core Web Vitals

Google doesn't just guess how good your website is. It measures user
experience using specific metrics called Core Web Vitals, and if your site
doesn't hit these benchmarks, your rankings and your sales will suffer.

Here is what Google is looking for.

### 1. Largest Contentful Paint (LCP)

This measures how long it takes for the main content of your page to fully
render on the screen. Anything over a 4-second delay needs immediate
improvement, and anything beyond that is considered poor performance.

### 2. Interaction to Next Paint (INP)

This measures your page's overall responsiveness to user interactions, like
clicks, taps, and keyboard input. An INP around 500 ms needs improvement, and
anything higher indicates a poor, laggy user experience.

### 3. Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS)

Have you ever tried to click a button, only for the page to suddenly shift and
make you click the wrong thing? That is what CLS measures. A score around
0.25 needs improvement, and anything higher means your page layout is too
unstable.

## How to fix it

The good news? You don't need to throw away your entire website. You likely
already have valuable content and great resources. Instead of a complete
rebuild, what you need is strategic optimization from a web design and
development expert.

Here are a few quick, highly effective ways to upgrade your page speed right
away:

- **Resize and compress images.** Never upload raw images. Compress them first
  to shrink the file size without losing quality.
- **Switch to next-gen formats.** Use WebP instead of JPEG or PNG. WebP files
  are significantly lighter and load much faster.
- **Ditch the GIFs.** GIFs are notorious bandwidth hogs. If you need
  animation, use short, optimized videos instead.
- **Modify your code.** Clean up and minimize your JavaScript, CSS, and HTML
  to streamline how your site loads.
